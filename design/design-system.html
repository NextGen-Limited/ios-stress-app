StressMonitor
Design System

The shared visual language powering 25 screens — five elemental companion characters, a five-level stress scale, instrument-panel precision for data, and Apple HIG chrome throughout. Every component here is extracted from production screens, not designed in isolation.

01 · Foundations

Color

Six neutral surfaces carry 80% of pixels. One accent (Ripple blue) appears at most twice per screen. The five-step stress scale is semantic, always paired with an icon or label. The character palette borrows from the stress scale where it can — Blossom's green echoes "Relaxed", Ember's coral echoes "High".

02 · Foundations

Typography

Three SF Pro families do all the work: Display for headlines, Text for body, Rounded reserved for character moments and big numbers (the otter speaks in rounded numerals). SF Mono carries tracked-uppercase metadata and tabular numerics. Letter-spacing is negative on display sizes (tightens big type), positive on uppercase meta (prevents counters from colliding).

03 · Foundations

Spacing & radii

The 20px content gutter (iOS standard) anchors every screen. Cards stack with 16px gaps. Grouped lists indent their margins by 16px from the screen edge. Hairline separators are 0.5px — Apple's UIScreen-accurate pixel weight — never 1px.

04 · Scaffold

iPhone frame

Every screen ships inside the same iPhone 14 Pro frame — 393 × 852pt, Dynamic Island, side-button rails, real SVG status-bar glyphs (signal/wifi/battery), and a home indicator. The frame is presentation chrome: it must stay pixel-identical across all 25 screens so a reviewer can diff layout without diffing chrome.

06 · Scaffold

Tab bar

Four roots, never more. Inactive tabs render a thin outline glyph in muted gray; the active tab fills the glyph in accent-strong and tints its label. Icons follow the SF Symbol .fill convention: every tab must ship a .fill-active variant that swaps to a solid silhouette on selection.

07 · Components

Buttons

Four button archetypes. Primary fills with Ripple blue and carries the screen's one decisive action. Secondary uses surface-2 with accent text for cancel/skip flows. Text is bare accent for inline links. Pill lives inside cards and lists for inline CTAs. Every button is at least 50px tall — above Apple's 44px tap target minimum.

11 · Components

Companion characters

13 · Components

Glass & surfaces

Frosted glass surfaces follow iOS Control Center lineage — backdrop-filter blur (14–20px) paired with strong saturation (165–180%) so colors stay vivid through the frost. Used sparingly: the tab bar, the premium banner on Home, and modal sheets. Never decorative.

Frost contract. Always pair blur with saturation ≥ 165%. Blur alone reads as muddy gray — saturation keeps the underlying color readable through the frost. iOS Control Center uses 30px blur + 180% saturation; we match that ratio at smaller sizes.

14 · Reference

Motion

Motion is restrained and functional. Three timings are used throughout: 150ms ease for color/state transitions on interactive elements, 200ms ease for toggle knob travel and modal sheet entry, 400–600ms ease for tide gauge fill and ring draw-on. All motion is suppressed under prefers-reduced-motion: reduce.

Reduced motion guarantee

The CSS rule below is in css/app.css. It zeroes out every animation and transition duration when the OS reports reduced-motion preference. No component needs to opt-in.

@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) {
  *, *::before, *::after {
    animation-duration: 0.01ms !important;
    animation-iteration-count: 1 !important;
    transition-duration: 0.01ms !important;
  }
}

15 · Reference

Accessibility

Five invariants that cannot regress. Stress colors always pair with an icon or label — never color-only. Tap targets are 44px minimum, 50px for primary CTAs. Body copy stays above 14px. Every interactive surface exposes data-od-id for screen-reader and inspector tooling. The home indicator and status bar glyphs are real SVG, not text stand-ins.
