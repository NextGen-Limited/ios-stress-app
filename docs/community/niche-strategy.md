## 1. Strategy at a glance

StressMonitor does not compete for the generic "stress app" keyword against 5M-user incumbents like StressWatch. We win by going **niche-deep**: we find the communities whose members already *know* their stress is real, measurable, and life-shaping — ME/CFS, ADHD, and burnout — and we give them the one thing every other stress app gets wrong: **a stress picture that reflects their whole physiology, not just HRV, shown as a face instead of a number that judges them.**

Three beliefs drive every post, comment, and DM in this playbook:

1. **Value first, product second.** We earn the right to be mentioned by being useful long before we link anything.
2. **The face is the feature.** "Your character looks tense today" lands differently than "Stress score: 72/100." We never lead with a number.
3. **We are a guest in their space.** These are support communities, not markets. Tone, pacing, and consent matter more than reach.

## 2. Why these three niches

| Community | Why StressMonitor fits | What they currently lack |
|---|---|---|
| **ME/CFS** (`r/cfs`) | Stress and energy crashes are central to their lived experience. PEM (post-exertional malaise) makes them hyper-aware of physiological state. Our 5-factor model (especially Recovery + Sleep) maps directly to the energy/stress balance they track daily. | A monitor that treats recovery and sleep as first-class signals — not optional extras bolted onto HRV. |
| **ADHD** (`r/ADHD`) | Emotional dysregulation, RSD (rejection sensitive dysphoria), and task-stress spikes are daily. They want *awareness without judgment* — noticing a pattern is half the battle. | A stress signal that doesn't gamify them into anxiety (no "bad score" shaming). Faces + breathing exercises = emotional regulation tool, not a leaderboard. |
| **Burnout** (`r/burnout`) | Chronic work stress, recovery debt, and sleep erosion define the group. They're actively looking for tools, often burned by apps that overpromise. | A whole-physiology picture (Sleep + Activity + Recovery) that shows the slow burn, plus breathing/walk interventions they can actually use in the moment. |

Two adjacent communities serve as **distribution amplifiers** rather than primary niches:

| Community | Role |
|---|---|
| **`r/AppleWatch`** | Hardware audience. They already own the sensor stack we read from. Best place to reach "I have an Apple Watch, what health apps are good?" threads. |
| **`r/StressManagement`** | Broad audience, lower intent, but good for general awareness and SEO of our positioning language ("5-factor, not just HRV"). |

## 3. Positioning: "5-factor stress monitoring, not just HRV"

### The core message

> Most stress apps read one number — HRV — and slap a score on it. StressMonitor reads **five** signals (HRV, heart rate, sleep, activity, recovery), learns your personal 30-day baseline, and shows you the result as a **character whose face changes with your stress** — never a number that judges you.

### The positioning triangle

```
                    ┌─────────────────────┐
                    │   "Not just HRV"    │  ← category claim
                    │   (5-factor model)  │
                    └──────────┬──────────┘
                               │
              ┌────────────────┼────────────────┐
              ▼                                  ▼
   ┌──────────────────┐              ┌──────────────────┐
   │  "No scores,     │              │  "Recovery &     │
   │   faces only"    │              │   sleep are      │
   │  (emotional)     │              │   first-class"   │
   └──────────────────┘              │  (physiological) │
                                     └──────────────────┘
```

- **Category claim** — "5-factor, not just HRV" differentiates us from every HRV-only app (StressWatch, Welltory, HRV+ etc.).
- **Emotional wedge** — "no scores, faces only" speaks to people who feel judged/gamified by wellness apps.
- **Physiological wedge** — "recovery and sleep matter" speaks to ME/CFS and burnout specifically, where HRV-only misses the story.

### Key differentiators (use these, in this order of priority)

| # | Differentiator | Why it matters to these niches |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **No scores — faces only.** 5 stress tiers shown as a character expression (😴 Resting → 😊 Calm → 😐 Balanced → 😰 Tense → 🐚 Overwhelmed). | ADHD + burnout: removes the anxiety of a "bad number." The character *is* the indicator. |
| 2 | **5 characters with evolution.** Ripple💧, Blossom🌿, Ember🔥, Zephyr🌬️, Lumi🌙 — each evolves Tamagotchi-style (Droplet → Ripple → Tidal) as you build healthy streaks. | All niches: turns stress *awareness* into a gentle, non-judgmental companionship loop. Recovery is rewarded, not punished. |
| 3 | **5-factor algorithm.** HRV + Heart Rate + Sleep + Activity + Recovery, weighted to a personal 30-day baseline with confidence scoring. | ME/CFS + burnout: recovery and sleep finally show up in the picture. Not just "your HRV dropped." |
| 4 | **Breathing exercises + biofeedback.** 4-4-4-4 box breathing with real-time feedback, plus Mini Walk with a circular timer. | All niches: an *intervention* lives next to the awareness. Most apps stop at the reading. |
| 5 | **Privacy-first.** Local SwiftData storage, E2E-encrypted CloudKit sync, HealthKit read-only, zero third-party analytics. | These communities are deeply private about health data. "Your data never leaves your devices" is a feature, not a footnote. |

### Anti-positioning (what we do NOT claim)

- We do **not** claim to diagnose, treat, or cure ME/CFS, ADHD, or burnout.
- We do **not** compare ourselves to medical devices or clinical HRV tools.
- We do **not** claim superiority over StressWatch on user count, ratings, or polish — only on the algorithm breadth and the no-scores philosophy.
- We do **not** promise "real-time" continuous stress (Apple's hardware samples HRV on ~15-min intervals; we're honest about this).

## 4. Reddit posting guidelines (value-first, not promotional)

Reddit communities — especially support communities — will ban self-promotion instantly. These rules are non-negotiable.

### The 90/10 rule (hard floor)

For every post or comment that *mentions* StressMonitor, produce **at least nine** purely value-adding contributions (answering questions, sharing research, empathizing) in the same subreddit. Track this ratio per subreddit.

### The "three gates" before you ever mention the app

Before mentioning StressMonitor in a subreddit, **all three** must be true:

1. **Account age & karma** — the account posting has ≥30 days age and meaningful karma *in that specific subreddit* (≥50 combined from that sub).
2. **Authentic context** — the app mention arises organically from the conversation (someone asked "are there apps that track recovery?", not you fishing for it).
3. **Consent-aware framing** — you frame it as *your* experience or build, never as a recommendation to the group ("I built / I've been using" beats "you should try").

### Tone rules

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Share what you learned building it | Claim it's "the best" |
| Be honest about limitations (e.g., "it's pre-launch, still rough") | Hype features as finished if they're not |
| Link to a non-promotional resource (a blog post, a research summary) | Link directly to the App Store in a support sub |
| Disclose you're the maker ("full disclosure, I'm the dev") | Astroturf — pretend to be an unaffiliated user |
| Ask the community what *they* want | Tell the community what they should want |

### Subreddit-specific rules (read the wiki before posting)

| Subreddit | Key rule to respect | Safe post type |
|---|---|---|
| `r/cfs` | Extremely protective of members; suspicious of anything that implies exertion/"just push through." **Never** frame stress monitoring as "you should be more active." | Research/question post: "How do you currently track your energy/stress state day to day?" |
| `r/ADHD` | Strict no-promotion. Mod approval required for any app. Members hate being sold "productivity cures." | Empathy/contribution: share a non-app coping mechanism; mention the app only if directly asked. |
| `r/burnout` | Open to tools but burned by hype. Values honesty about burnout being systemic, not an "app problem." | "Show & tell" of a build journey (people love maker stories). |
| `r/AppleWatch` | Welcomes "what app does X?" answers. Self-promo allowed in a weekly thread or with disclosure. | Reply in "weekly app recommendation" threads; standalone post only with dev disclosure + genuine value. |
| `r/StressManagement` | Moderate. Welcomes research-backed discussion. | Educational post: "What 5 physiological signals actually predict stress (with sources)" — app as footnote. |

### Crisis protocol

If a community member posts about self-harm, suicidal ideation, or acute crisis:

- **Never** respond with a product mention. Ever.
- Share crisis resources (local hotlines, the sub's pinned resources).
- Report the post to mods per that sub's protocol.
- This is a hard line. A single product mention in a crisis thread permanently destroys trust in that community.

## 6. Metrics & success signals

We do **not** measure success in installs during month one. We measure:

| Signal | Target (month 1) | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Positive karma in each priority sub | ≥100 per sub | Proof we're adding value, not noise |
| Organic mentions by others | ≥3 | Others recommending us = real traction |
| TestFlight signups from Reddit | 50-150 | Engaged early users, not vanity installs |
| DMs / feedback threads | ≥10 conversations | Qualitative signal for product direction |
| Mod relationship status | No warnings/bans in any sub | We're a good guest |
| Negative signal: ban or warning | 0 | Hard fail if this happens — pause & reassess |

### What to do if you get warned or banned

1. **Stop immediately** in that subreddit.
2. Message mods respectfully — acknowledge, ask what you did wrong, do **not** argue.
3. Wait for their response before any further activity.
4. Document it and adjust the playbook. A ban in r/cfs or r/ADHD is a serious brand hit — protect these relationships above all.

## 7. Beyond Reddit (phase 2, out of scope for this playbook)

Once the Reddit foundation is solid (month 2+):

- **Discord communities** — several CFS and ADHD Discords exist; same value-first rules apply, often stricter.
- **App Store reviews** — early Reddit users who became fans are your most authentic reviewers. *Ask privately, never incentivize.*
- **Maker communities** — Indie Hackers, Hacker News (Show HN when there's a technical angle: the 5-factor algorithm, the on-device privacy model).
- **Content** — a blog/video on "why we removed stress scores" is evergreen positioning content that ranks for the niche queries.

## 8. Quick-reference message bank

Short, honest, reusable phrases. Adapt — never copy-paste verbatim across subs.

**On why faces, not scores:**
> "A number tells you you're failing. A face just tells you how you're doing. I wanted the latter."

**On 5-factor vs HRV-only:**
> "HRV alone misses the story for a lot of people — if your sleep is wrecked and you haven't recovered, that's stress too, even if your HRV looks 'fine.'"

**On recovery for CFS:**
> "Recovery and sleep are first-class signals in how it calculates stress, not optional extras — because for energy-limited conditions, those are the whole picture."

**On privacy:**
> "Everything stays on-device and syncs E2E-encrypted through iCloud. No analytics, no ad IDs. Health data is read-only — we never write to Apple Health."

**On honest limitations:**
> "It's pre-launch and rough in places. Apple's hardware only samples HRV every ~15 minutes, so it's not truly continuous — I'd rather be honest about that than overpromise."

**On the maker disclosure:**
> "Full disclosure: I'm the developer. I'm sharing because [genuine reason tied to the thread], not to push it on anyone."
